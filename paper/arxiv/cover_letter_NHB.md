# Cover letter — Nature Human Behaviour

Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, "A scaling law of contextual persistence in human language," for publication as an Article in *Nature Human Behaviour*.

I report the discovery of a scaling law of human language: the influence of prior context on each word, isolated from the contribution of word content, decays as an approximate 1/d power law with distance — mean exponent α = 1.04 across ten corpora spanning six language families and both written and spoken modalities. Using large language models purely as measuring instruments, the contextual persistence function P(d) captures the dimension of language in which grammar and meaning live — the arrangement of words in sequence — where existing laws (Zipf's, Heaps') describe words only in aggregate. The law is a property of the human communicative signal itself: a quantitative behavioural regularity, measured at corpus scale, that yields directly testable predictions for reading behaviour and neural response.

The near-unity exponent is the heart of the finding: α ≈ 1 is the unique boundary at which contextual influence is distributed approximately equally across logarithmic timescales — a specific organization of influence across scales, not a generic heavy tail. The law vanishes in scrambled and synthetic controls, replicates across independent probe models, and does not appear in genomic or protein sequences under domain-native models. It completes a measurement program begun with Shannon's guessing experiments and Hilberg's conjecture, in which prediction was measured as a function of context length but order itself was never a variable. And it makes specific behavioural and neural predictions — reading-time and N400 costs of context disruption falling off at the near-unity rate — converging with model-free power-law measurements in memory research.

The manuscript is ~4,200 words with five figures. A preprint will be posted to arXiv. All code and derived data are available for review and will be deposited with a DOI upon acceptance. This work is not under consideration elsewhere, and I declare no competing interests.
